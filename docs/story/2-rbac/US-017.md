### US-017 — Extend last-admin lockout protection to admin-equivalent custom roles

### User Story
As a platform security owner,
I want the last-admin lockout guard (US-012 AC5) and `RbacZeroActiveAdminsHealthIndicator` to recognize admin-equivalent custom roles, not just the literally-named `TENANT_ADMIN`,
So that a tenant cannot be silently zeroed out of administrative access by revoking a custom role that happens to carry `role:write`/`user:write`/`tenant:write`.

### Background / Context
This story exists to close **RES-3**, a residual risk accepted out of scope by both US-016 (Gate 1 #8) and, before it, implicitly assumed away by US-012/US-015. AC5's lockout guard and `RbacZeroActiveAdminsHealthIndicator` (`docs/features/US-016/03-design.md` §12.3 RES-3) both test membership in the literal `TENANT_ADMIN` role only. US-016 gates `assign()`/`revoke()` by actual privilege, not name — which means a tenant's *de facto* administrators can now legitimately hold a custom role instead of `TENANT_ADMIN` — but the lockout guard and the health indicator were not extended to match. A tenant whose sole active administrator holds an admin-equivalent custom role (rather than literal `TENANT_ADMIN`) can have that role revoked with no last-admin protection and no health-indicator signal: the tenant is silently zeroed out of admin-equivalent access.

US-016's design strengthens the case for fixing this rather than creating the gap: §6.4's corollary shows the ≥1-admin invariant is now enforced *more* strongly for the literal `TENANT_ADMIN` role precisely because US-016 narrows its reachable revocation population to self-revocation — an asymmetry that becomes sharper, not smaller, if the admin-equivalent custom-role case is left unguarded indefinitely.

**Paired with RES-9** (`docs/features/US-016/03b-threat-model.md`, `docs/features/US-016/03-design.md` §12.3): RES-9 notes the caller-side admin test in US-016's own gate remains name-based (tests literal `TENANT_ADMIN`) while the target-side test is privilege-based, so a custom-role holder with all three dangerous permissions can be *subject to* the gate but can never *pass* it. Both RES-3 and RES-9 turn on the same underlying design question — should any caller/holder-side check in this subsystem become privilege-based rather than name-based — and are recorded as **one Epic-3 question**, not two independent stories, so that whichever design resolves one is evaluated against the other before either ships.
