### US-016 — Gate role assignment/revocation by actual privileges, not role name

### User Story
As a platform security owner,
I want role assignment and revocation (`RoleAssignmentService.assign()`/`revoke()`) to check the *privileges* a role actually confers, not just whether its name matches `TENANT_ADMIN`,
So that a custom role built via US-015 and later renamed, or any role a `TENANT_ADMIN` grants dangerous permissions to, is gated the same way the seeded `TENANT_ADMIN` role is.

### Background / Context
This story exists to close the "propagate" half of a privilege-escalation chain that US-015 deliberately left open as an accepted residual risk (R-3/D15). US-015 (`docs/features/US-015/03-design.md` §10, `docs/features/US-015/03b-threat-model.md` §4.5) closes the "mint" side: attaching a dangerous permission (`role:write`, `user:write`, `tenant:write`) to *any* role now requires the caller to hold an active `TENANT_ADMIN` assignment (AC11).

What remains open: `RoleAssignmentService.assign()`/`revoke()` (US-012 AC8) still gate solely by matching the target role's *name* against `TENANT_ADMIN` — verified in `RoleAssignmentService.java:190-260` — not by whether the role being assigned actually carries admin-equivalent privileges. A role named anything other than `TENANT_ADMIN` that has been granted `role:write`/`user:write`/`tenant:write` (a legitimate, admin-gated action per US-015 AC11) can then be assigned to any user through the ordinary, non-admin-gated assignment path, handing that user admin-equivalent power without ever tripping AC8's check.

US-015's Gate 2 accepted not closing this gap in that story's scope, because a real fix touches `assign()` and `revoke()` — a shipped authorization contract — and raises design questions (what counts as "admin-equivalent"? all three dangerous permissions, or a broader set?) that shouldn't be decided inside an already-scoped story. This story is the tracked successor that was the condition of accepting that risk (C3 in Gate 2's threat model sign-off).

**This risk acceptance has a hard expiry at Epic 3 kickoff** — Epic 3 makes attaching dangerous permissions to custom roles a routine, expected admin action, which invalidates the premise that exploitation is rare/detectable. This story must be prioritized before Epic 3 begins, or R-3 must be re-reviewed.
